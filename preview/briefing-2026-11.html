Payments Compliance Weather · November 2026

The forecast for payments compliance

A record-scale U.S. anti-money laundering penalty, a new payments network targeted for sanctions evasion, card scheme deadlines landing, and Europe’s new payments rulebook one vote away. Fraud remains the dominant theme, now with an explicit focus on scam compounds and the forced labor behind them.

Stormy

Fraud and scams

Fraud is now the global standard-setters’ top priority, and new liability rules are coming in Europe.

October 30, 2026Global

FATF puts scam compounds at the center of its fraud work

At its October 28–30 plenary in Paris, the first under the UK presidency, the FATF approved a report on the end-to-end illicit financial flows linked to scam compounds, with publication expected shortly after. It also removed Bulgaria, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Monaco from its list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring, and announced a targeted review of countries with significant virtual asset sectors.

Why it matters

The FATF presidency has made fraud its flagship priority, estimating that fraud costs around $500 billion a year and noting it was the main predicate offence in most recent country evaluations. Expect supervisors to follow its lead.

What to do

Update country risk ratings for the four delisted jurisdictions in line with your policy, and plan to review the scam compound report against your fraud and money mule typologies when it is published.

Sources

  • ComplyAdvantage summary of FATF October 2026 plenary
  • AML Intelligence on the FATF president’s fraud plan

October 29, 2026European Union

EU payments rulebook (PSD3 and PSR) adopted by the Council

The Council of the EU confirmed it has adopted the texts of the third Payment Services Directive and the new Payment Services Regulation at first reading. The European Parliament still needs to adopt them in plenary before publication in the Official Journal.

Why it matters

The package brings mandatory checks that the payee’s name matches the IBAN, makes providers liable for losses when their fraud controls are inadequate, and requires refunds in certain impersonation fraud cases. Implementation periods follow publication.

What to do

Start a gap analysis now on payee verification, fraud monitoring and impersonation fraud refunds, and assign owners for the transition plan.

Sources

  • Regulation Tomorrow, Nov 2026
  • DLA Piper on the PSD3 and PSR provisions

Unsettled

Card network rules

Mastercard’s recurring-payment linking rule took effect on October 23, alongside new European scheme fees.

October 23, 2026Global

Mastercard requires the original Transaction Link Identifier on recurring charges

From October 23, 2026, recurring and other merchant-initiated Mastercard transactions are expected to carry the Transaction Link Identifier (TLID) returned on the customer’s first transaction. Processor guidance differs on the consequences of missing it, from higher issuer declines to possible fees and weaker evidence in disputes.

Why it matters

Subscription merchants that store cards in a separate vault or billing engine are most at risk of dropping the identifier.

What to do

Confirm with your processor that the TLID is stored and passed on every subsequent charge, and check how missing identifiers are being handled.

Sources

  • Summary of processor guidance on the TLID rule

October 1, 2026Europe, UK and Canada

New scheme fees take effect this autumn

Changes effective in October include a Visa consumer funding fee on certain account funding transactions in Europe (October 1), a Mastercard fee on preauthorizations that are not cleared or reversed within 30 days in Europe (October 1), a revised Mastercard authorization exemption indicator fee in Europe and the UK (October 12), and a new Visa acquirer network access fee in Canada (October 19).

Why it matters

Fees tied to authorization practices, such as unreleased preauthorizations and exemption flags, reward good transaction hygiene and penalize poor practices.

What to do

Ask your acquirer for the exact rates that apply to you, and review preauthorization release and exemption flagging processes.

Sources

  • PayPal card network updates: Fall 2026

Stormy

Regulatory enforcement

A $1.2 billion FinCEN penalty and coordinated action against the A7 payments network.

October 13, 2026United States

FinCEN fines Bank of America $1.2 billion over anti-money laundering failures

FinCEN assessed a $1.2 billion penalty against Bank of America, N.A. The bank admitted it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act, including failing to maintain an adequate anti-money laundering program, failing to conduct due diligence on correspondent accounts for foreign financial institutions, and failing to file suspicious activity reports. The OCC imposed a separate $750 million civil money penalty, and OFAC settled apparent sanctions violations for $11.6 million.

Why it matters

FinCEN tied the failures to priority national security risks, including Chinese money laundering networks and illicit finance along the southwest border. Regulators are judging programs on whether they detect and report the risks named in the national priorities, not only on paperwork.

What to do

Map your monitoring scenarios and correspondent due diligence to FinCEN’s published priorities, and test whether alerts on those risks actually lead to timely SAR decisions.

Sources

  • FinCEN press release, Oct 13, 2026

October 1, 2026United States

Treasury targets the Russia-linked A7 payments network and its sub-agents

OFAC designated the A7 Network as a significant transnational criminal organization. On the same day, FinCEN issued a finding and proposed rule under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act that would bar covered financial institutions from sending or receiving funds, including convertible virtual currency, involving A7’s sub-agents. The draft names six UAE-based sub-agents. FinCEN also issued an alert with red flags (FIN-2026-Alert007).

Why it matters

A7’s sub-agents are third-country companies used so that the real customer does not appear in the payment chain. This is a pattern payment firms, correspondents and crypto businesses can miss with name screening alone.

What to do

Screen for the named sub-agents, review the alert’s red flags against trade and cross-border payment flows, and use the SAR key term FIN-2026-A7NETWORK where relevant. The proposed rule is not yet final; comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication.

Sources

  • FinCEN notice of proposed rulemaking, Oct 1, 2026
  • FinCEN alert on the A7 Network

Changeable

Consumer protection

U.S. open banking rules are on hold, while the UK reviews its scam reimbursement scheme.

July 1, 2026United Kingdom

UK scam reimbursement: first-year review shows lower losses, consultation due in December

An independent evaluation published by the Payment Systems Regulator found that authorised push payment (APP) scam losses over Faster Payments fell by about 21%, around £73 million a year, after mandatory reimbursement began in October 2024, and the share of losses reimbursed rose from 54% to 65%. The PSR plans a formal consultation in December 2026. Separately, the Financial Services and Markets Bill would move the PSR’s functions, including reimbursement, into the FCA.

Why it matters

The requirement is here to stay, but its scope and administration may change, and the supervisor is changing.

What to do

Prepare evidence on your reimbursement outcomes and scam prevention for the December consultation, and track the Bill’s progress through the House of Commons.

Sources

  • Regulation Tomorrow on the PSR evaluation, Jul 1, 2026
  • Status of the Financial Services and Markets Bill

June 8, 2026United States

U.S. open banking rule remains on hold

The CFPB’s personal financial data rights rule under section 1033 is finalized on paper but preliminarily enjoined while the Bureau reconsiders key areas, including third-party access, data security, privacy and whether banks may charge fees. The original first compliance date of April 1, 2026 passed without taking effect.

Why it matters

Data access, fees and liability for data-sharing remain uncertain, so many firms are relying on private agreements in the meantime.

What to do

Keep data-sharing agreements flexible, and watch for a new proposed rule with revised deadlines.

Sources

  • Open Banking Tracker: Section 1033 status (last reviewed Jun 8, 2026)

Watch

Human crimes

FATF has approved a report on the money flows behind scam compounds, where trafficked people are forced to run scams.

May 11, 2026United States

Human trafficking red flags in payments data

FinCEN’s notice ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup (FIN-2026-NTC1) listed financial indicators of human trafficking that remain useful beyond the tournament, including late-night travel and hotel spending, bulk purchases of prepaid cards, rapid peer-to-peer transfers from new accounts that are then forwarded on, and gas-station ATM activity between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m.

Why it matters

Payment apps, prepaid programs and card issuers often see these patterns first. Linked with FATF’s work on scam compounds, which rely on trafficked labor, human trafficking increasingly overlaps with fraud.

What to do

Check that your peer-to-peer and prepaid monitoring covers these indicators, and train investigators to consider trafficking when they see scam and money mule patterns.

Sources

  • FinCEN notice FIN-2026-NTC1

On the horizon

  • UK PSR consultation on APP scam reimbursement, expected in December 2026.
  • Publication of FATF’s report on illicit financial flows from scam compounds.
  • Comment deadline on FinCEN’s proposed A7 sub-agent prohibition, 30 days after Federal Register publication.
