Human crimes

The human cost behind the transaction

Every alert, dispute and suspicious payment may involve a person: someone trafficked into a scam compound, a child being extorted, an older adult losing their savings. Payments data often shows these crimes before anyone else does.

Four crimes we focus on

Human traffickingUsing force, fraud or coercion to make someone work or provide commercial sex for someone else’s profit. Child sexual exploitationAny sexual abuse or exploitation of someone under 18, in person or online, including grooming and sextortion. Elder financial exploitationThe illegal or improper use of an older adult’s money, property or assets, most often by someone they trust. Predatory scamsSchemes that trick people into handing over money or personal information themselves.

For compliance teams

May 11, 2026United States

Human trafficking red flags in payments data

FinCEN’s notice ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup (FIN-2026-NTC1) listed financial indicators of human trafficking that remain useful beyond the tournament, including late-night travel and hotel spending, bulk purchases of prepaid cards, rapid peer-to-peer transfers from new accounts that are then forwarded on, and gas-station ATM activity between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m.

Why it matters

Payment apps, prepaid programs and card issuers often see these patterns first. Linked with FATF’s work on scam compounds, which rely on trafficked labor, human trafficking increasingly overlaps with fraud.

What to do

Check that your peer-to-peer and prepaid monitoring covers these indicators, and train investigators to consider trafficking when they see scam and money mule patterns.
