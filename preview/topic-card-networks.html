Topic

Card network rules

Scheme rules, monitoring programs, fees and data requirements that merchants, acquirers and payment facilitators must meet to keep accepting cards.

Unsettled in the November 2026 forecast. Mastercard’s recurring-payment linking rule took effect on October 23, alongside new European scheme fees.

Latest from the briefing

October 23, 2026Global

Mastercard requires the original Transaction Link Identifier on recurring charges

From October 23, 2026, recurring and other merchant-initiated Mastercard transactions are expected to carry the Transaction Link Identifier (TLID) returned on the customer’s first transaction. Processor guidance differs on the consequences of missing it, from higher issuer declines to possible fees and weaker evidence in disputes.

Why it matters

Subscription merchants that store cards in a separate vault or billing engine are most at risk of dropping the identifier.

What to do

Confirm with your processor that the TLID is stored and passed on every subsequent charge, and check how missing identifiers are being handled.

October 1, 2026Europe, UK and Canada

New scheme fees take effect this autumn

Changes effective in October include a Visa consumer funding fee on certain account funding transactions in Europe (October 1), a Mastercard fee on preauthorizations that are not cleared or reversed within 30 days in Europe (October 1), a revised Mastercard authorization exemption indicator fee in Europe and the UK (October 12), and a new Visa acquirer network access fee in Canada (October 19).

Why it matters

Fees tied to authorization practices, such as unreleased preauthorizations and exemption flags, reward good transaction hygiene and penalize poor practices.

What to do

Ask your acquirer for the exact rates that apply to you, and review preauthorization release and exemption flagging processes.

Frameworks to know

Dispute and fraud monitoring

Visa’s Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP) combines fraud and disputes into one ratio. From April 1, 2026, the excessive threshold for merchants fell to 1.5%, with fees per fraudulent or disputed transaction for enrolled merchants.

Scam merchants

Mastercard updated its standards on merchants engaged in scams in 2026, requiring faster investigation by acquirers once risk thresholds are reached and immediate termination where scam activity is confirmed.

Recurring payments

From October 23, 2026, Mastercard expects recurring and other merchant-initiated transactions to carry the original Transaction Link Identifier.

Card data security

PCI DSS version 4.0.1 is the current data security standard. Its future-dated requirements became mandatory on March 31, 2025.

Summaries are general information. Check the primary rules for your jurisdiction and business model.
