Topic

Fraud and scams

Authorized push payment fraud, impersonation, investment and romance scams, money mules, and the rules that decide who pays when a customer is tricked.

Stormy in the November 2026 forecast. Fraud is now the global standard-setters’ top priority, and new liability rules are coming in Europe.

Latest from the briefing

October 30, 2026Global

FATF puts scam compounds at the center of its fraud work

At its October 28–30 plenary in Paris, the first under the UK presidency, the FATF approved a report on the end-to-end illicit financial flows linked to scam compounds, with publication expected shortly after. It also removed Bulgaria, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Monaco from its list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring, and announced a targeted review of countries with significant virtual asset sectors.

Why it matters

The FATF presidency has made fraud its flagship priority, estimating that fraud costs around $500 billion a year and noting it was the main predicate offence in most recent country evaluations. Expect supervisors to follow its lead.

What to do

Update country risk ratings for the four delisted jurisdictions in line with your policy, and plan to review the scam compound report against your fraud and money mule typologies when it is published.

October 29, 2026European Union

EU payments rulebook (PSD3 and PSR) adopted by the Council

The Council of the EU confirmed it has adopted the texts of the third Payment Services Directive and the new Payment Services Regulation at first reading. The European Parliament still needs to adopt them in plenary before publication in the Official Journal.

Why it matters

The package brings mandatory checks that the payee’s name matches the IBAN, makes providers liable for losses when their fraud controls are inadequate, and requires refunds in certain impersonation fraud cases. Implementation periods follow publication.

What to do

Start a gap analysis now on payee verification, fraud monitoring and impersonation fraud refunds, and assign owners for the transition plan.

Frameworks to know

Authorized vs. unauthorized fraud

In authorized fraud the customer makes the payment themselves after being deceived, so traditional unauthorized-transaction protections often do not apply. This is why new reimbursement and liability rules focus on scams.

United Kingdom

Mandatory reimbursement of authorised push payment (APP) scam losses over Faster Payments has applied since October 2024, with costs shared between sending and receiving firms. The Payment Systems Regulator’s functions are planned to move into the FCA.

European Union

The new Payment Services Regulation (PSR) and third Payment Services Directive (PSD3) bring payee name checks, provider liability where fraud controls are inadequate, and refunds for certain impersonation fraud. Final adoption is in progress.

Global standards

The FATF has made fraud a strategic priority, with particular attention to scam compounds and the digital services that enable them.
