Payments Compliance Weather, November 2026

November 2026 edition

Selected developments: FinCEN’s $1.2 billion penalty against Bank of America, FTC actions against Riverside Payments and Nuvei, and Visa’s reduced VAMP merchant threshold. This is not a complete list.

Published November 5, 2026. Covers developments from October to early November 2026

Card network rules

In effect since April 1, 2026Asia Pacific, Canada, Europe and the United States

Visa’s VAMP merchant threshold

Visa’s Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP) measures card-not-present fraud reports and disputes against settled transactions in a single ratio. Visa’s fact sheet sets acquirer thresholds at 0.5% (above standard) and 0.7% (excessive). On April 1, 2026, the excessive threshold for merchants fell from 2.2% to 1.5% in Asia Pacific, Canada, Europe and the United States, for merchants with at least 1,500 fraud reports and disputes a month.

Why it matters

In aggregated setups, such as merchants of record and PayFacs, one seller can drive the ratio while the overall account still looks healthy.

What to do

Track fraud reports and disputes per seller, not just per account.

Sources

  • Visa: VAMP fact sheet
  • Visa Perspectives: VAMP program update

Regulatory enforcement

November 2, 2026United States

FTC and Washington act against a payment processor over small-business sales practices

The FTC and the State of Washington alleged that Riverside Payments, its affiliate Cascade Equipment Leasing and their operators misled small businesses with savings claims, incomplete quotes, buried auto-renewal and early termination terms, and a hard-to-use cancellation guarantee. The stipulated order requires a $600,000 payment, with a $32.1 million judgment suspended, and requires clear disclosure, express consent for auto-renewals and a simple way to cancel. These are allegations.

Why it matters

Deception and auto-renewal principles are being applied to how processors sell to businesses, not only to consumers.

What to do

Review merchant-facing quotes, savings claims, renewal terms and cancellation flows.

Sources

  • FTC press release, Nov 2, 2026

October 13, 2026United States

Bank of America: $1.2 billion FinCEN penalty, with OCC and OFAC actions

FinCEN assessed a $1.2 billion penalty against Bank of America, N.A. The bank admitted willful Bank Secrecy Act violations, including failing to maintain an adequate AML program, to conduct due diligence on foreign correspondent accounts and to file required Suspicious Activity Reports. The OCC assessed a $750 million civil money penalty, and OFAC announced an $11.6 million settlement over apparent sanctions violations.

Why it matters

Coordinated actions by several agencies raise the cost of a single program failure.

What to do

Test whether your monitoring would catch, and report, the patterns named in the action.

Sources

  • FinCEN press release, Oct 13, 2026

September 4, 2026United States

Nuvei settles FTC claims over processing for deceptive merchants

The FTC alleged that Nuvei opened and kept accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were engaged in deception, including more than $30 million in payments for the Reimage tech support scheme. The order requires $4.85 million for consumer redress, bans processing for telemarketed or pop-up tech support sellers and tactics such as load balancing, and requires screening and monitoring of clients.

Why it matters

The case sits alongside the FTC’s 2025 action against Paddle, which also involved Reimage.

What to do

Check your controls against load balancing, terminated-merchant re-entry and chargeback-driven escalation.

Sources

  • FTC press release, Sep 4, 2026
