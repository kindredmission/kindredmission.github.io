Why I Started Kindred

By the founder of Kindred, October 24, 2026. Updated November 5, 2026. 5 minute read

Let me start with a confession.

I work in payments compliance. My days are spent looking for what others miss: the pattern that doesn’t add up, the red flag tucked inside an ordinary file. I also help build the controls and products that are meant to catch those things before anyone gets hurt. Noticing is, quite literally, my job. So I thought I was pretty good at seeing things.

In early 2026, I was invited to a learning session on human trafficking. I assumed I already understood the subject, at least well enough. By the end of that session, I realized I didn’t.

The picture I’d carried in my head was wrong. I had imagined strangers, kidnappings, people smuggled across borders in the dark. What I heard instead was quieter, and much closer to home. Trafficking can happen without anyone being moved at all. It can happen in a business we walk past every day, to someone who once said yes to a job that sounded like a way out. And sometimes the people being harmed don’t recognize it either, because these crimes are designed to look like something else: an opportunity, a relationship, a favor.

If someone whose whole job is to notice things had been looking right past this, how many of us were?

What I started to see in my own work

That question changed how I read my own field.

Earlier in my career, I worked on card brand investigations. Whether a business was a merchant, a merchant of record or a payment facilitator came up again and again, and it was rarely a straightforward answer. In payments compliance we talk in classifications, thresholds, ratios and audit findings. That precision is necessary. But it can make us forget what those decisions are for.

A classification decides who is underwritten, whose activity is monitored, and whether a seller is visible to the acquirer and the card network at all. When a harmful seller goes unseen, the cost does not land on a risk register. It lands on a person: the older adult who paid for fake technical support, the family charged for a subscription they never agreed to, the small business locked into a contract it didn’t understand.

The same is true of the crimes that first opened my eyes. Human trafficking, the sexual exploitation of children, the financial exploitation of older adults, and scams that prey on loneliness, fear and hope all have one thing in common. Sooner or later, they need a way to move money. That puts payments professionals in a rare position to notice.

Why Kindred

That’s why I started Kindred.

I chose the name because it is about kinship: seeing the person on the other side of the transaction as someone’s daughter, someone’s father, someone’s friend. As our kin.

Kindred exists to make the human impact of payments decisions impossible to overlook, so that awareness becomes responsibility, and responsibility becomes action.

For payments and compliance professionals, that means Payments Compliance Weather, a discussion of select card network topics and enforcement actions, along with practical guides and open conversation across the industry. The goal is to look past labels and checklists to the real risks. For everyone, it means keeping the human crimes behind the money in view: what they look like, and where to get help.

I don’t have all the answers. Payments compliance is full of grey zones, and I’m still learning, the same way I hope you will. But I know that no single company, team or regulator sees the whole picture. Together, we see more.

So I’m asking you to join me with your piece. If you work in payments, challenge the analysis, share what you see in your own work, and bring these conversations to your team. If you don’t, learn the signs, check on your people, and ask the questions that feel awkward.

Behind every payment is a person. It’s time we kept them in view.

With hope,
The founder of Kindred
