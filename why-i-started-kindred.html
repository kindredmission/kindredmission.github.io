Why I Started Kindred

By the founder of Kindred · October 24, 2026 · 5 minute read

Let me start with a confession.

I work in compliance. My days are spent looking for what others miss: the pattern that doesn’t add up, the red flag tucked inside an ordinary file. I also help build the controls and products that are meant to catch those things before anyone gets hurt. Noticing is, quite literally, my job. So I thought I was pretty good at seeing things.

In early 2026, I was invited to a learning session on human trafficking. I almost didn’t think twice about it. I assumed I already understood the subject, at least well enough.

By the end of that session, I realized I didn’t.

The picture I’d carried in my head for years, the one I suspect many of us carry, was wrong. I had imagined strangers, kidnappings, people smuggled across borders in the dark. What I heard instead was quieter, and so much closer to home. Trafficking can happen without anyone being moved at all. It can happen in a house down the street, in a business we walk past every day, to someone who once said yes to a job that sounded like a way out.

Something shifted in me that day. It wasn’t just shock. It was a fire. If someone whose whole job is to notice things, and to build the systems that notice them, had been looking right past this, how many of us were?

So I started reading. Late at night, page after page, trying to understand what I’d missed. And one thing stopped me cold. It wasn’t only that most of us don’t understand these crimes. Sometimes the people being harmed don’t recognize it either.

Think about a young woman who believes the man controlling her is her boyfriend. A worker who thinks the debt he can never pay off is simply what he owes. A grandmother who trusts the kind voice on the phone, call after call, until her savings are gone. A teenager who has been told, again and again, that this is normal, that this is what love looks like, that no one would believe her anyway.

Some people don’t realize they’re about to be exploited. Some don’t realize they already are. Not because they aren’t smart or careful, but because these crimes are designed to look like something else: an opportunity, a relationship, a favor, a family obligation.

I remember sitting with that and thinking, how sad is that? To be hurt, and not even have the words for what’s happening to you. To be surrounded by people, and still be completely unseen.

Human trafficking. Child sexual exploitation. The financial exploitation of older adults. Scams that prey on loneliness, fear and hope. These crimes are insidious. They hide in ordinary places, and they are very likely happening somewhere in our own communities right now. And yet we rarely talk about them.

I understand why. These topics make us uncomfortable. It’s easier to believe they happen somewhere else, to someone else. So we change the subject, we assume someone else is handling it, and we stay quiet. But silence is exactly what these crimes need to survive.

Here’s the truth I can’t shake: this could be any of us. It could be your parents, trusting the wrong caller. It could be your children, talking to someone online who seems to understand them better than anyone else. It could be you. It could be me.

Why Kindred

That’s why I’m starting Kindred.

I chose the name Kindred because this movement exists to awaken the humanity in us: the part of each of us that sees the person being exploited down the street as someone’s daughter, someone’s father, someone’s friend. As our kin.

Kindred is here to help our community understand what these crimes really look like, to talk about them openly instead of in whispers, and to replace myths with facts. To make sure that people who are being harmed are seen, believed and connected to help. And to overcome, together, the discomfort that keeps us silent.

I don’t have all the answers. I’m still learning, the same way I hope you will. But I know this: no single person, agency or organization can stop exploitation alone. Community can.

So I’m asking you to join me. Not with something enormous, just with your piece. Raise your voice: share what you learn, and start the uncomfortable conversation at your dinner table, at work, in your group chat. Give your time, even an hour. Learn the signs, and help one more person learn them too. And check on your people. Call your parents. Talk with your children. Ask the questions that feel awkward.

Each of us holds one building block. On its own, it may not seem like much. Together, it can become something no exploiter can hide behind.

The humanity is already in us. It’s time to wake it up.

With hope,
The founder of Kindred

Kindred

Kindred is a community movement to awaken the humanity in us and help people recognize and stand against human trafficking, child sexual exploitation, elder financial exploitation and predatory scams. It is not a crisis service, a law enforcement agency or a legal provider.
